A Florida-based, veteran-owned consultancy that helps public agencies establish where they stand against the Title II deadline and decide what to fix first. The deliverable is an Accessibility Conformance Report: every WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA success criterion marked and evidenced, dated and signed. Delivered by one principal with an enterprise data and automation background.

Services

Title II Readiness Assessment

Automated scanning plus manual sample testing, including keyboard and screen reader checks, against WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Review of the published accessibility statement, coordinator designation, and grievance procedure against what the rule requires. Deliverable is an Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR) in VPAT 2.5 format, the same document you already require from your software vendors, plus a prioritized roadmap with effort bands and a one-page summary suitable for a board or budget request. Two to four weeks.

Document Inventory & Triage

Catalog every PDF and document across the agency's web properties, then classify each one: retire, replace with an accessible HTML page, remediate, or leave. Remaining items are prioritized by page traffic, program criticality, and exposure. Deliverable is a scoped work order a remediation vendor can execute against, so per-page remediation spend goes to documents that are actually in use.

Program & Policy Setup

Accessibility statement naming the standard and conformance level, Title II coordinator designation, grievance procedure, self-evaluation documentation, procurement language requiring a VPAT or ACR before purchase, and content governance so newly published material stops regressing.

Ongoing Monitoring

Recurring scans, quality review on newly published content, and annual reassessment against the standard.

Differentiators

  • No product to sell. No overlay, no platform license, no per-page remediation quota. The recommendation follows the finding.
  • Evaluation only, never the remediation. A conformance claim written by whoever built or repaired the site is self-assessment. Staying out of the repair work is what lets the report mean something, and it gives you a specification your remediation vendor bids against rather than a per-page quote you have no way to check.
  • A tagged PDF is not a conforming PDF. Most checkers ask only whether tags exist. A document can have them and still be unreadable: no headings, tables with no header cells, images with no alt text. That is what an auto-tag pass leaves behind, and often what an agency receives after paying to have it fixed.
  • The report says what it did not check. Reading order, meaningful alt text, and focus order are not machine-detectable, and asserting conformance on automated evidence alone is how a report becomes the exhibit. An unanswered criterion reports as Not Evaluated, never as Supports.
  • Triage before spend. Most agencies are about to pay per-page rates on documents that are obsolete, duplicated, or should never have been PDFs. Sorting that first is the cheapest money in the program.
  • Built for volume. An enterprise data and automation background applied to cataloging and prioritizing document libraries that run to five figures.
  • Florida presence. In Punta Gorda, on-site as needed, and certified in the Florida vendor pool while the national firms work remote.
  • Set-aside vehicle. Certified Veteran Business Enterprise for state, county, municipal, and university preference.

Demonstrated Capability

  • Conformance report issued: a full evaluation of brianbeals.com against all 50 WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA success criteria, remediated to conformance and documented in an Accessibility Conformance Report in VPAT 2.5 format. Every defect found was fixed and re-verified, including a skip link that scrolled the page without moving keyboard focus.
  • Purpose-built evaluation tooling: an assessment engine combining automated rule checking with the checks automation normally misses, among them 320-pixel reflow, dark color scheme contrast, and skip links that scroll the page without moving keyboard focus. Documents are read rather than counted: the tag tree is walked to find missing alt text, tables with no header cells, and tagged files with no headings at all. A regression suite runs against fixture pages encoding known defects, so the engine is tested rather than trusted.
  • Volume screening in practice: 22 Florida public entities verified and sampled across four counties, producing per-entity findings mapped to specific success criteria. Third-party widget defects are attributed to the vendor rather than to the entity, so an agency is never handed a finding it cannot act on.
  • Large-scale content inventory: automation that polls a government opportunities API, filters and classifies records by code and keyword, scores them, and publishes a live dashboard. The same pattern applied to a document library.
  • Public GIS integration: pulls live FWC and FL DEP ArcGIS feature services, runs spatial analysis, and publishes a hosted feature layer plus a public interactive map, rebuilt automatically via CI.
  • Scheduled pipelines: data screeners running on a schedule via GitHub Actions and publishing to public web endpoints without manual intervention.
  • Modern web delivery: a Next.js site on Vercel with CI/CD deploys, plus multiple public sites maintained under version control.

Method. Findings are measured, not estimated. Where a check cannot be made honestly, for example text over a photograph where the background color is unknowable from the page, it is reported as indeterminate rather than asserted. A finding a client can disprove costs more than a finding never made.
