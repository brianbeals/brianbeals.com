Summary ▼

The same 49-slide, four-chapter capability series, now carrying the v2 critique's corrections: slide 30 uses the anchored claim slide 47 demonstrates, the slide 13 filename no longer wraps mid-extension, footers match the revision date, the "X, not Y" pattern is down from four instances to one natural one, and slide 33 names what the series proves for a hiring reader. Slide 2's four cards were expanded from the author's overview PDF and now carry full value statements instead of taglines.

The structural fixes landed. What this pass found is the residue of the expansion itself: ported copy that brought its own baseline numbers and point of view with it.

Professionalism A

The contradiction is gone and the chrome is right. Slide 30 now practices what slide 47 preaches, the filename console reads cleanly, the folder map matches the real convention, and every footer carries June 10. The design system holds across all 49 slides; the verification renders on slides 2, 13, 30, 31, and 33 are clean, including the line break that keeps slide 30's longer title clear of its number badge.

The manual baseline is now quoted three ways. Slide 2 says a plan "took a week," slide 23 says "takes days," slide 30 says "took two days." Each is defensible alone; together they read as a number that bends to the sentence. This arrived with the ported overview copy. Pick one baseline and let every slide quote it.
Slide 2 breaks the deck's point of view. "This is the reason the other three sound like Brian" is third person on a slide whose neighbors say "who you are" and "your voice." The overview PDF was written about Brian; the deck talks to the reader. One pronoun fixes it.

Writing Style A−

Flesch-Kincaid: roughly grade 6–7, unchanged and right for the format. The cadence repairs hold: "in week one" and the rewritten slide 31 card read as claims rather than templates, and the single remaining "degree, not pass or fail" on slide 46 is the natural kind.

The glance slide got wordier. Slide 2's cards went from one-line taglines to four-line value statements. The new copy is good prose, and it fits the boxes, but the slide's job is a ten-second scan, and it now asks for forty. This is the one place v3 traded punch for completeness; the redline trims each card back toward two lines without losing the new substance.
No banned vocabulary, no em-dashes, fragments still capped at pairs. The ported copy kept the voice rules even though it came from a different artifact, which is the system doing its job.

Leadership Capability A

The ask now exists. Slide 33's added line tells a hiring reader what the series proves. Positions are still taken and owned, the offer to demo live still stands, and the chapter structure still does the reader's work for them.

"One person's working system" sits next to two names. The new slide 33 sentence claims individual authorship while the slide credits both Brian and Steve below it, and slide 1 says "built together." The deck's own answer, one system per person, each on their own profile, is a stronger leadership claim anyway: it says the system scales without flattening anyone. The redline makes that explicit. This reviewer wrote the sentence in question, so weigh the finding accordingly.

AI Authorship Assessment 30%

Standard signals: clean, and now uncontradicted. Concrete specifics, owned positions, varied rhythm, zero AI vocabulary across 49 slides. On v2, the counter-detection check fired twice (ironic self-contradiction plus curated specifics) and the floor rule forced the score to 45%. With slide 30 fixed, only curated specifics remains; one signal is noise by the scoring rule, so the score returns to the standard-signal band.

Reported: 30%, likely human-led with disclosed AI assistance. Slide 1 says the deck was built with the assistant from Brian's profile, so the number confirms rather than reveals. Disciplined writing under explicit style rules is indistinguishable from well-prompted AI; this deck is the documented case of that, on purpose.

Snarky Meter 12%

Unchanged from v2. "Road Runner, prospect (unreachable)" and the 70-years-pre-revenue Avian Capture division are still the sharpest lines, and they still punch at a cartoon. The new slide 2 and slide 33 copy added warmth, no edge. Nothing to soften.
