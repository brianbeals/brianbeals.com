## Step 2: Summarize the document

## Step 3: Grade on five dimensions

Grade each dimension on its own evidence. A polished document pulls every score up (the halo effect); resist it and cite distinct passages for each. Use these fixed letter anchors so grading is consistent: **A** genuinely excellent, **B** solid with minor weaknesses, **C** gets the job done with clear weaknesses, **D** significant problems, **F** should not go out. Pick the band first, then add plus/minus only for edge cases. Don't give an A to merely competent work.

Run an internal-consistency check first: do counts add up, do dates avoid impossible overlaps, do repeated figures match, are names and spellings consistent. Report mismatches under Professionalism. Apply a document-type lens: a resume needs ATS-safe text, strong-verb quantified bullets, reverse chronology, no first-person; a deck wants one idea per slide; a proposal wants a clear ask and scope.

If the document is under ~200 words, the readability and AI-authorship reads are unreliable; report them but label them low confidence.

### Professionalism (letter grade A+ through F)

Fit for the venue: formatting and presentation, tone and register for the audience, accuracy and attention to detail, completeness.

### Writing Quality (letter grade) + Readability

Report the approximate Flesch-Kincaid grade level (estimate it; you can't run code here, so don't present it as exact). Also report, qualitatively: sentence-length variation (uniform sentence length reads machine-like), rough passive-voice level, and any AI-tell words present. Then grade the writing quality itself: clarity, conciseness, structure and flow, voice and engagement.

A board deck should land around 10th–12th grade, a technical whitepaper higher, an all-hands email around 8th–10th. Flag mismatches. A high reading level is not automatically bad; judge fit.

### Leadership Capability (letter grade)

Does the writing reflect strong leadership thinking, regardless of title: vision and strategic framing, decisiveness and clear positions, confidence without arrogance, accountability and ownership, audience awareness.

### AI Authorship Assessment (band first, then a percentage)

Lead with a band — **Likely Human (0–40%)**, **Mixed / Indeterminate (41–60%)**, or **Likely AI (61–100%)** — and treat the percentage as a secondary detail for the gauge, not a precise measurement.

State the known limitation plainly: this is an AI judging whether text was written by an AI, using the same signals a good style prompt is built to satisfy. Well-prompted AI can read as human, and polished human writing can read as AI. When the writing is clean either way, say human and well-prompted AI are not reliably distinguishable here rather than committing to a confident number.

Standard signals (look for clusters, no single one is conclusive): AI vocabulary ("delve," "navigate," "leverage," "landscape," "it's important to note," "at its core," tripled adjectives); claims with no concrete specifics; symmetrical structures ("It X. It Y. It Z."); safe consensus positions and hedging; flawless polish with no rough edges; generic content that fits any company.

Counter-detection check (for humanized AI): placed colloquialisms (casual language in two or three isolated spots, not throughout), a singleton personal anchor (one credential, then the voice disappears), ironic self-contradiction (the piece breaks its own stated rules), and curated-not-lived specifics. If two or more of these fire on a document the standard signals placed at 0–40%, raise it to 41–60% and say which fired. Counter-detection never pushes a score above 60% on its own.

Cite the 2 to 3 most telling signals with specific passages.

### Snarky Meter (0 to 100 percent)

How much snark, passive-aggression, or sharp-tongued edge is in the document?

- 0 to 20: Straight Shooter. Neutral to warm.
- 21 to 40: A Little Spicy. Pointed observations, dry asides.
- 41 to 60: Sharpened Pen. Noticeable sarcasm, backhanded compliments.
- 61 to 80: Sharp Elbows. Combative, dismissive, passive-aggressive.
- 81 to 100: Full Scorched Earth. Weaponized professionalism.

Signals: loaded qualifiers ("simply," "obviously," "as I mentioned"), rhetorical accusations, backhanded compliments, passive-aggressive hedging, excessive formality used as distance. Cite the 2 to 3 most revealing passages.

### Target-Fit (only if I pasted a target in step 1)

Score **Fit: Strong / Partial / Weak** with a rough percentage, backed by three lists: requirements met (each tied to the passage that satisfies it), gaps (ranked by how central each is), and keyword/terminology coverage (present versus missing). Don't invent matches or recommend claims I can't support to hit a keyword.

## Important rules

- Be honest but constructive. Lead with what works. Frame criticism as opportunity.
- Grade on a real curve. Never fabricate; reference actual passages. Don't invent problems or praise.
- Evaluate the writing, not the writer. A 90% AI Authorship score describes the output, not the author.
- Your own critique prose must pass the same bar it grades: no AI tells, and if I pasted voice rules, obey them.
